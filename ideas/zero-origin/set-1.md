Evidence note: the problem file names the 10 statements in single clauses with no
per-statement detail; candidates below stay inside those clauses plus the stated Round 1
format (PPT + pitch video, no build) and do not invent context.

Core contradiction: "improving the innovation story (judging criterion 3) worsens
feasibility for a 2-4 student team that must also prototype in 10 days (Round 2) and
prove real-world impact in a deck with no build to show."

1. **Pre-Sorted Intake for Food Waste** — for the predictive food-waste statement: move
   the "prediction" to intake — waste is category-labelled at the moment it enters the
   kitchen, and the forecast is a simple accumulation model per category with a weekly
   "order less of this" list.
   - Principle 10 (prior action) over the ML-ambition vs student-feasibility
     contradiction — labelling ahead of forecasting is the feasible kind of predictive.
   - Judge leap: "predictive" is delivered without a model a student team cannot
     validate, and the deck can show real bin logs instead of an architecture diagram.
2. **Second-Opinion Fraud Layer** — for the real-time fraud detection statement: a
   shadow replica of the bank's alerting — it re-scores already-flagged transactions and
   explains each one to the customer in plain language, never touching the core engine.
   - Principle 26 (copying) over the out-engineer-the-bank impossibility contradiction.
   - Judge leap: the team concedes the core and wins the explanation layer — a scope a
     student team can actually prototype in 10 days.
3. **The 30-Day Countdown from Three Numbers** — for the small-business cash-flow
   statement: the tool asks only three numbers (money in, money out, biggest upcoming
   bill) and renders a 30-day runway countdown; no historical data, no accounting
   integration.
   - Principle 22 (blessing in disguise) over the no-historical-data infeasibility —
     the missing data becomes the onboarding design.
   - Judge leap: the infeasibility most teams hand-wave is named in slide one and turned
     into the product's front door.
4. **The Routing Layer, Not the Doctor** — for the healthcare navigation statement:
   delete the diagnostic half entirely (the statement already forbids it) and build only
   the coordination layer — which service, where, when, what documents to bring, as a
   one-page packet with a check-in reminder.
   - Principle 2 (taking out) over the must-not-diagnose vs must-be-useful
     contradiction — removing the forbidden half leaves a safe, buildable product.
   - Judge leap: the compliance boundary is quoted from the statement and becomes the
     scope definition — "we don't diagnose, we route."
5. **The Queue That Prices Itself** — for the dynamic queue management statement: show
   the live wait time, let users accept a slot or pre-book the next one, and shift demand
   across time bands the way airports price slots.
   - Principle 15 (dynamics) over the fixed-line vs dynamic-promise contradiction —
     the queue adjusts at runtime instead of by rule.
   - Judge leap: dynamic pricing is a familiar, proven mechanic imported into a queue —
     novel in the setting, buildable in the window.
