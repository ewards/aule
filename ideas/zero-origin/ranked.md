## Recommendation

1. **Pre-Sorted Intake for Food Waste** (set-1, total 17) — it is the only candidate
   that beats the field on the first listed criterion (problem validation): real bin
   logs from the team's own college kitchen, a solution small enough to prototype in
   Round 2's 10-day window, and "predictive" delivered without a model a student team
   cannot validate. Kill-risk: one kitchen is a small impact story until the
   unit-economics slide generalizes it — that slide is load-bearing.
2. **The App That Asks Three Questions a Day** (set-2, total 16) — the freshest shape in
   a field of dashboards: the forecast stops depending on the business remembering to log
   and the system acts on the user instead, which answers both "innovative" and
   "understanding of affected users" in one demo beat (the 10-second ask/answer loop).
   Kill-risk: three daily pings read as spam if the loop is not visibly under ten
   seconds; the pitch video must show the loop, not describe it.
3. **The Pilot Letter** (set-3, total 16) — a named institution on video saying what it
   will pilot replaces the TAM slide as the impact proof, which is the hardest Round 1
   criterion for a student deck to fake and the easiest to verify; it layers on any of
   the product candidates above. Kill-risk: a student-collected letter of intent can read
   as a formality — the institution and the person on video must be specific.

Method note: no cross-domain (set-4) or inversion (set-5) candidate reached the top 3.
The cross-domain best (The Discharge Packet, rank 4) is the strong pick if the team
chooses the healthcare statement instead of food waste or cash-flow; the inversion
candidates are deck discipline (Name the User on Slide One, Two Weeks of Data Before
the Deck) that should be folded into whichever product is chosen rather than submitted
as the idea.
