Evidence note: the 10 problem statements are single clauses in the problem file; the
mappings below stay inside those clauses (food waste, dynamic queues, healthcare
navigation, scholarship matching) without inventing per-statement context.

Core loop, domain-neutral: a small, low-budget team must close a loop an organization
runs badly every day (waste, queues, navigation, matching) — with evidence, not
promise.

1. **The Discharge Packet** — source: hospital operations, the discharge packet plus the
   48-hour check-in call.
   - Mapping: for the healthcare navigation statement, every routed service ships as a
     one-page packet — what, where, when, documents to bring, what to expect — with one
     check-in reminder, delivered over WhatsApp/SMS.
   - Kill risk: a packet is a document, not a product; without a delivery channel the
     deck shows paper, and the "automation" claim evaporates.
2. **Slot Pricing for the Waiting Room** — source: airport operations, slot times and
   stand assignment.
   - Mapping: for the dynamic queue statement, the queue becomes a slot market — live
   wait times shown, users accept a slot or pre-book the next, and demand shifts across
   time bands exactly as airport slots shift.
   - Kill risk: "paying in time" invites a fairness objection in judging (elderly,
     emergency) that the deck must answer or it reads as cold.
3. **Waste as Line-Item Inventory** — source: kitchens and food service, mise en place —
   every component logged at the pass before service.
   - Mapping: for the food-waste statement, waste is logged per line item at intake and
   at discard, and the output is a weekly "order less of this" reorder suggestion —
     waste managed as inventory discipline, not as a forecasting model.
   - Kill risk: the data only works if logging happens; the pitch must show the log
     taking under five seconds per item or the habit claim fails.
4. **Underwriting the Scholarship** — source: insurance underwriting, structured
   questionnaire to risk class to price.
   - Mapping: for the intelligent scholarship matching statement, the student answers a
   structured eligibility questionnaire, each scholarship is "underwritten" into a match
   score, and the output is a ranked list with a gap note per shortcoming criterion.
   - Kill risk: the catalogue of scholarships must exist and be current — the underwriting
     model is only as good as its policy table, and a student team owns that data burden.
