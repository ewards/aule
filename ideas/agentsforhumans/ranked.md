## Recommendation

1. **The Decision Inbox** (set-1, total 16) — the problem statement's tightest constraint ("runs autonomously and only surfaces when there's a real decision") becomes the entire product: the human interface is a stream of approve/defer/reject cards, and the demo's center beat is a day where the agent did everything but two taps. Kill-risk: if the cards look like push notifications with extra steps, Design reads as thin — each card must carry enough context (why, what, when, undo) to be a real decision surface.
2. **The Simulated World** (set-3, total 16) — a five-minute time-lapse of thirty days of autonomous operation (advance one day per click, real Strands agent, seeded world) is the strongest demo mechanic in any set: it is the easiest end-to-end story to follow and the impact evidence the criteria ask to be demonstrated. Kill-risk: the simulated frame must be stated in the first 30 seconds — "real agent, seeded data" — or a judge reads the whole build as fake.
3. **The Standing Order** (set-3, total 16) — the redefinition play: the product is the trust contract (standing orders granted once) plus its violation log, which is the most non-obvious Strands use in this run and a concrete artifact a judge can hold; it directly answers the "background autonomy needs accountability" tension. Kill-risk: a contract page is abstract until lived-in — it needs 8-10 standing orders with real history and at least one logged near-miss, or it reads as a policy document.

Just under the line (all 16s): Reversible-First Engine, The Power of Attorney, The 86 List, and Zero-Open Guarantee — note they compose with the top 3: the Decision Inbox is the surface, the Reversible-First Engine is its safety architecture, and the 86 List is its hard-stop layer; The Power of Attorney and The Standing Order are near-duplicates (scope-as-product) — pick one.

Method note: no SCAMPER (set-2) candidate reached the top 3 — its candidates were solid support mechanisms (onboarding, meters, sentinels) rather than headline concepts, which may mean SCAMPER bends best around a chosen core here rather than producing one; its Front-Desk Agent for a Food Bank (15) remains the best Good-Neighbor-track entry if a different track is wanted.
